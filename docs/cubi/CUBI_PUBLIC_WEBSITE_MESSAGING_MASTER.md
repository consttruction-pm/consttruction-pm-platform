## 1. Positioning

CUBI is a professional project-controls and delivery platform for organizations that plan, engineer, build, operate and maintain complex projects. It brings project scope, work breakdown structures, schedules, relationships, baselines, progress, resources, cost, documents, contracts, risks, reporting and decisions into a connected, traceable workflow.

CUBI is designed for the full project lifecycle—from early definition and planning through execution, monitoring, handover and operational close-out. It supports project teams without assuming that every organization works in the same way: project structures, calendars, languages, permissions and reporting contexts must remain configurable and auditable.

## 2. Industries and project environments

The public message must speak to more than building construction. CUBI is relevant to:

- Building construction, civil engineering and infrastructure.
- Oil, gas and petrochemical projects: upstream facilities, wellsite and gathering infrastructure, pipelines, terminals, refineries, petrochemical plants, tank farms, offshore facilities and brownfield shutdowns/turnarounds.
- Energy and utilities: power generation, renewables, transmission, distribution, water and wastewater.
- Industrial, manufacturing and process facilities.
- Engineering, Procurement and Construction (EPC) organizations, owners, operators, contractors, consultants and project-management teams.

Industry examples describe intended use contexts; they must not imply that every specialist operational, engineering-design, safety, process-control or asset-management function is already implemented in CUBI.

## 3. Audiences

Write for the people who make projects work:

- Owners, investors, executives and portfolio leaders who need reliable visibility and accountable decisions.
- Project directors, project managers, PMO teams and project-controls managers.
- Planning and scheduling engineers who manage WBS, activities, logic, calendars, baselines, critical paths and updates.
- Cost engineers, estimators, quantity surveyors and commercial teams.
- Construction, site, field and commissioning managers.
- Resource, procurement, contracts, document-control, risk, quality and reporting specialists.
- EPC contractors, engineering consultancies, subcontractors and delivery partners.
- Small and growing teams that need disciplined project controls without enterprise-level complexity.

Avoid language that suggests CUBI is only for large corporations or only for construction-site users.

## 4. Core capability narrative

Describe the platform as connected capabilities, not as a list of disconnected modules:

1. Project definition and structure — organize portfolios, projects, disciplines and work breakdown structures.
2. Planning and scheduling — build and maintain activity networks, calendars, relationships, baselines, milestones and schedule updates.
3. Project controls — connect scope, time, progress, performance indicators, change and decision records.
4. Resources and cost — organize resource demand, assignments, budgets, actuals and cost visibility where the relevant project services are enabled.
5. Documents and contracts — keep controlled records, revisions, evidence and commercial context linked to project work.
6. Field and delivery information — bring site updates, issues, inspections, equipment and daily records closer to project controls where supported.
7. Reporting and analytics — produce traceable project views, reports and exports from governed project data.
8. Engineering Intelligence Assistant and Smart Guide — help users find relevant CUBI guidance, understand project information and prepare proposed actions. It must respect permissions, cite its knowledge basis where available, disclose uncertainty and request approval before changing project records.
9. Collaboration and governance — support role-based access, review, auditability, revision control and clear accountability.
10. Flexible access — provide a consistent product experience across supported web, desktop and mobile clients, with offline behavior only where the relevant client capability is implemented and verified.

## 7. Claims and terminology guardrails

- Do not claim that CUBI replaces engineering-design, process-control, ERP, accounting, document-management or asset-management systems unless a specific integration or capability is implemented and verified.
- Do not claim autonomous AI, generative answers, predictive accuracy, guaranteed outcomes or automatic project changes. Use “Engineering Intelligence Assistant”, “Smart Guide”, “guided assistance” and “proposed actions” where accurate.
- Do not claim offline support for the web product. Describe offline capability only for client workflows that have been implemented and tested.
- Do not imply that every feature is enabled for every tenant, plan, project or deployment.
- Do not expose customer data, sample data as real customer evidence, confidential source material or external product branding.
- Keep CUBI terminology consistent across every locale. Translate meaning naturally; do not translate product names, identifiers, WBS, EPC, CPM, EVM or standard acronyms where retaining the acronym improves clarity.
- Preserve right-to-left direction for Persian and Arabic; use locale-aware typography, dates, numbers and punctuation.
