# Multi-project budget and concurrency evidence — P0-N.2

## 1. Mechanism

`GlobalExecutionBudget` (`src/construction_pm/scheduling_execution_budget.py`) is the one
process-wide limit on how many slots may be used at once. A slot is what a project may
use for its own float workers: a project holding three slots runs one parent and at most
two float children, so the sum over concurrent projects never exceeds the budget.

`GlobalWorkerScheduler` (`src/construction_pm/scheduling_worker_scheduler.py`) dispatches
projects without containing scheduling logic:

* `SlotAllocationPolicy` grants at least one slot per project, orders small jobs first so
  they are not trapped behind large ones, and gives a lone large project every free slot.
* There is no preemption; a running project keeps its slots until completion, and the
  next project starts from the next completion.
* Each project's result is hashed independently, so concurrent execution cannot change
  another project's output.

The durable worker uses the same budget (`SchedulingWorkerRuntime` creates
`GlobalExecutionBudget(settings.slots or cpu_count)`), so API execution and benchmark
execution are bounded by the same mechanism.

## 3. Measured capacity and project sweeps

The real measurements are in `performance-benchmarks.md` and the raw
`results/` JSON. They are generated by `scripts/p0n2_runtime_matrix.py` on the host
recorded in `environment.json`:

* capacity: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 slots at size 25,000 mixed;
* projects: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 concurrent 5,000-activity projects at budget 4, with every
  project hash compared to its own sequential hash.

### 3.1 Capacity (25,000 mixed, one engine run per point)

| slots | engine ms | CPU s | speedup vs 1 | hash == 25k mixed cell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 6,551.1 | 6.54 | 1.000 | yes |
| 2 | 6,589.0 | 6.59 | 0.994 | yes |
| 4 | 3,836.8 | 7.25 | 1.707 | yes |
| 8 | 3,736.1 | 7.11 | 1.753 | yes |
| 16 | 3,777.3 | 7.16 | 1.734 | yes |

The step is between 2 and 4 slots; above 4 (the host's CPU count) wall time stops
improving while CPU seconds stay flat, which is the expected saturation on a 4-CPU
host, not a budget failure. Every point produced the same output hash as the
orchestrated 25,000 mixed cell.

### 3.2 Projects (5,000 mixed each, `GlobalExecutionBudget(4)`)

| projects | wall ms | throughput (proj/s) | max queue wait ms | peak slots | process starts | hashes match |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1,088.9 | 0.918 | 0.1 | 4 | 1 | yes |
| 2 | 1,637.9 | 1.221 | 2.5 | 4 | 2 | yes |
| 4 | 1,675.4 | 2.387 | 4.1 | 4 | 4 | yes |
| 8 | 3,345.4 | 2.391 | 1,669.0 | 4 | 8 | yes |
| 16 | 6,605.7 | 2.422 | 4,959.5 | 4 | 16 | yes |

Throughput rises until four projects are in flight and then plateaus at ~2.4
projects/s, which is the 4-CPU budget, while `peak_slots` never exceeds 4. The queue
wait is the visible fairness signal: a project submitted into a backlog of 8 or 16
waits 1.7 s and 5.0 s respectively before its first slot, and every project still
completes with its own sequential hash. No project starves and no project exceeds the
budget.

Nothing in this file is extrapolated. If a cell could not complete, it is recorded as an
error in the raw JSON and repeated verbatim in `performance-benchmarks.md`.
