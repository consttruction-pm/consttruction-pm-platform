# CUBI Project Management Academy — Step-by-Step Curriculum

**Curriculum ID:** CUBI-PMA-01  
**Version:** 1.0.0 foundation  
**Sources:** The companion source register in this directory.  
**Delivery:** Document and interactive Smart Guide only; no video dependency.

## 1. Learning outcome

Teach the learner to initiate, define, plan, authorize, execute, monitor/control, change, hand over and close a construction or industrial project. Every lesson connects management reasoning to a deliverable and the corresponding real CUBI workspace operation.

The assistant is a CUBI-owned deterministic command guide, not an open-ended chatbot. The learner selects a preferred level (beginner, practitioner or specialist); the system does not infer credentials from writing style. It asks only material missing-input questions, never invents project facts, and cites source/version/page anchors.

## 2. Continuous synthetic case

Use one segregated training case: **Riverside Pump Station Upgrade**. The case includes engineering, site preparation, civil/structural work, mechanical/electrical installation, instrumentation and controls, testing, commissioning and handover.

Every example date, duration, quantity, crew size, productivity rate, cost, currency and assumption must be labelled **SYNTHETIC TRAINING DATA**. Never reuse those values in a live project or present them as industry norms. Real projects use only authorized project files and confirmed inputs.

## 3. Sequential curriculum

| No. | Module | Management question and learning outcome | Main exercise / output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00 | Project context and intake | What type of project, discipline, lifecycle, jurisdiction, delivery model, contract and calendar are involved? What is known or missing? | Set EPS/project discipline first, then project name and project ID; create a context and missing-input checklist. |
| 01 | Need, feasibility and business case | Why do the project, what benefits/outcomes are expected, what options exist and what assumptions affect viability? | Need statement, options appraisal, feasibility and business-case outline. |
| 02 | Governance and organization | Who owns, directs, delivers, assures and approves the project? | Governance plan, organization, RACI/responsibility matrix, authority and stage-gate log. |
| 03 | Stakeholders and requirements | Who needs what, how will requirements be validated and changes traced? | Stakeholder and requirements registers, acceptance criteria, interfaces and communication plan. |
| 04 | Scope and deliverables | What is included, excluded and accepted? | Scope statement, deliverable list, exclusions, assumptions, boundaries and acceptance criteria. |
| 05 | WBS and work packages | How is all scope decomposed into manageable deliverables and control accounts? | Multi-level WBS, WBS dictionary, coding, work-package boundaries and responsibility links. |
| 06 | Schedule and network logic | What work occurs, in what sequence, under which calendar and constraints? | Activity list, milestones, FS/SS/FF/SF logic, lag/lead justification, calendars, constraints, CPM and logic review. |
| 07 | Duration and productivity | What evidence supports durations, quantities, production rates and uncertainty? | Estimate basis, quantity/productivity assumptions, work-time conversion, ranges and schedule risk inputs. |
| 08 | Resources and construction methods | Which people, equipment, materials, methods, capacities and work fronts are required? | Resource dictionary, roles, crews, equipment, materials, calendars, assignments and leveling review. |
| 09 | Cost estimating and budget | How is the estimate built, what is included and how is cost time-phased? | Estimate basis, quantities, unit rates, direct/indirect costs, escalation, contingency, cash flow and cost baseline. |
| 10 | Procurement and contract strategy | What must be bought, when is it required, who supplies it and which obligations govern? | Procurement plan, packages, RFQ comparison, commitments, long-lead register, contract and payment milestones. |
| 11 | Risk, issues and opportunities | What uncertain events or current issues could affect objectives and what response is justified? | Risk breakdown, cause-event-effect, analysis, owner, trigger, response, contingency, issue and assumption logs. |
| 12 | Quality, HSE and environment | How will work comply with requirements and be shown safe and acceptable? | Quality plan, ITP, inspections/tests, NCR/corrective action, HSE controls, permits and environmental controls. |
| 13 | Documents, information and BIM | Which revision is current, who owns it, how is it approved and linked to work? | Document register, drawings/specifications, RFI/submittals, revision/transmittal and information-exchange controls. |
| 14 | Integration and baselines | How do scope, time, resources, cost, risk and commitments form one controlled plan? | Integrated baseline review, schedule/cost baselines, assumptions, traceability and change thresholds. |
| 15 | Execution and status collection | What was actually performed, when, by whom and with what evidence? | Data date, actual start/finish, remaining duration, physical/quantity progress, labor/equipment hours and status evidence. |
| 16 | Monitoring, forecast and EVM | Are we on plan, what is the forecast and what explains variance? | CPM/criticality/float review, variance analysis, approved EVM/earned-schedule measures, forecast and corrective actions. |
| 17 | Change, delay and claims | What changed, what is the cause/effect, what approvals/notices apply and what evidence is needed? | Change request, impact analysis, delay-event log, contemporaneous evidence, notice/entitlement review and claim dossier. |
| 18 | Recovery and reforecast | Which recovery alternatives are feasible and what are their time, cost and risk trade-offs? | Resequencing, extra work fronts, resource/shift options, procurement acceleration and controlled scenario comparison. |
| 19 | Testing, commissioning and handover | How is operational readiness proven and accepted? | System completion, tests, punch list, training, as-builts, O&M, warranties, spares and acceptance certificates. |
| 20 | Closeout and benefits | Have contractual, technical, financial, information and operational obligations been closed? | Final account, claims/change status, completion evidence, archive, lessons, benefits review and closure approval. |

## 4. Standard lesson design

Each lesson must include:
1. Objective and prerequisites.
2. Plain-language explanation, then optional specialist-level detail.
3. Why the management step exists and what decision it controls.
4. Required inputs, source, data type, unit and missing-input behavior.
5. Ordered procedure and responsible role.
6. Worked example with every invented value marked SYNTHETIC TRAINING DATA.
7. Relevant CUBI page ID, command ID, typed record fields and supported application operation.
8. Expected outputs and links to related project artefacts.
9. Formula contract if applicable: variables, units, assumptions, applicability, limits, rounding and deterministic tests.
10. Common errors, validation checklist and failure cases.
11. Practical task and deterministic assessment rubric.
12. Exact source IDs, edition, page/section/table/figure pointers.
13. Approval warning where a proposed action could change real project data.

## 5. Example lesson: build an activity network

1. Confirm the approved scope and WBS deliverable before naming activities.
2. Gather the construction method, quantities, productivity basis, work calendar, interfaces, constraints and acceptance conditions.
3. Break the work into measurable activities with clear start/finish conditions and accountable parties.
4. Add predecessor/successor logic. Choose relationship type and lag/lead only with a stated physical, technical or contractual reason.
5. Check open ends, dangling logic, excessive hard constraints, summary links and calendar conflicts.
6. Run the authoritative Shared Domain/Calculation Core on the complete persisted project revision.
7. Review early/late dates, total/free float, criticality and milestone feasibility against actual project facts.
8. Explain assumptions, unresolved inputs and alternative sequences.
9. Present typed proposed changes and require normal permission checks and user approval before any write.
10. Capture source anchors and validation evidence in the lesson result.

## 6. Assessment and safeguards

- Grade management understanding separately from UI operation, using deterministic rubrics and units.
- Do not equate agreement, popularity or repetition with technical correctness.
- Passing a lesson never mutates the live project.
- Keep learner progress in user-private or explicitly authorized tenant scope.
- A completed academy is not a professional certification.
- If a capability is not implemented, explain the concept but state that the CUBI operation is unavailable; no mock persistence or fake success.
- Smart Guide must navigate the one canonical Visual Workspace; no second help/chat product may replace it.
- Localized lessons must preserve semantic IDs, units, dates and calculation meaning.

## 7. Acceptance

A learner must be able to complete the synthetic case from intake to closeout using only CUBI's own deterministic command and knowledge capabilities. Every lesson must have source/version/page anchors, a validation rubric, missing-data rules, permission-aware proposed actions and privacy-safe progress persistence. All numerical outcomes must come from the Shared Core where relevant.
